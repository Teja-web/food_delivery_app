# Dilliznt: The Complete Story

## The One-Liner

**Dilliznt is an AI-powered talent operating system that builds, improves, and deploys talent — powered by a persistent memory layer that compounds over time.**

---

## Part I: The Business Case

### The Problem

Hiring today is a **stateless, fragmented system**. Candidates restart every application. Companies lose context across roles and tools. Evaluation signals don't carry forward. Recruiters spend 80% of their time on mechanical work — screening resumes, writing boolean searches, coordinating interviews, maintaining spreadsheets — instead of the judgment calls that actually matter.

The result: wasted effort, poor matching, and zero compounding intelligence.

> Hiring is a network with **no memory.**

### The Core Insight

**Talent is not static — it evolves over time.**

Existing systems treat candidates as a resume, a score, a pipeline stage. But real talent is skills that grow, preferences that shift, and history that compounds. Every interview, assessment, and interaction generates signal — and today's tools throw it all away the moment a req closes.

### What Dilliznt Is

Dilliznt is an **AI-powered talent operating system for RPO companies** that replaces the fragmented hiring stack with a single intelligence layer. It doesn't just track candidates — it profiles them, scores them, matches them, coaches them, interviews them, assesses them, and learns from every interaction to make the next hire faster and better.

### The Positioning: RPO, Not SaaS

Dilliznt is **not a software vendor**. There's no per-seat license. No ATS replacement pitch.

**Software is our infrastructure. Outcomes are our product.**

Revenue comes from:
- **Managed hiring (RPO)** — we deliver hires, not dashboards
- **Role fulfillment** — pay-per-outcome aligned with client success
- **Talent development programs** — college pipelines, training cohorts, internship-to-hire funnels

This means our incentives are perfectly aligned: we only win when the client gets the right hire.

### Why This Wins

| Advantage | What It Means |
|-----------|---------------|
| **Compounding data** | Every candidate interaction improves future matching — the system gets smarter with scale |
| **Supply control** | College partnerships and talent development create candidates, not just find them |
| **Evidence chains** | Explainable AI decisions (scores, alignment, gaps with sources) build trust with hiring managers |
| **Persistent memory** | Candidates carry forward across roles and time — nothing is lost |
| **Outcome alignment** | RPO model means we eat our own cooking — bad matches hurt us, not just the client |

### The Defensibility

Traditional ATS and SaaS competitors can't replicate this because:

1. **The memory layer** — persistent candidate intelligence that compounds across roles, clients, and time
2. **The talent graph** — 26 specialized AI agents building interconnected intelligence, not a single LLM bolted onto a database
3. **The supply pipeline** — college and institutional partnerships that create pre-qualified talent before it hits the open market
4. **The evidence hierarchy** — observed interview behavior > tested assessment scores > AI-scored profiles > self-reported resume claims. Every recommendation comes with a chain of evidence, not a black-box score
5. **The outcome model** — SaaS vendors optimize for seats sold. We optimize for hires made. Different incentives produce a fundamentally different product.

## Part II: The Product — From Role to Hire (and Beyond)

### Act 1: Define the Need

**A role opens.** A recruiter pastes a JD or describes the role in plain language. The **Role Wizard** agent extracts structured fields — competencies, must-haves, compensation range, team context. Interview stages are configured with competency weights and linked assessments.

The moment the role is saved:
- The rules engine fires `role:insert`
- **Role Intake** enriches missing fields and generates hiring intelligence
- **Match Evaluation** scores every candidate in the system against this role
- **Pool Matching** identifies which talent pools align and surfaces pre-qualified candidates

The recruiter opens the role page to a pre-ranked pipeline — before they've sourced a single candidate.

---

### Act 2: Build the Supply

Candidates enter the system through six interconnected channels:

- **Resume upload** — parsed, profiled, and talent-scored automatically
- **Portal self-registration** — candidates maintain a living profile
- **Public opportunities funnel** — strangers browse anonymized roles at `/opportunities` (k-anonymous bucketing protects org identity until a candidate consents to sign up). The "front door" of the platform — no auth required, SEO-indexed, OG-imaged, and one click from anonymous browse → application
- **Vendor sourcing network** — partner staffing agencies operate as vendor orgs with their own login. Per-role visibility (`internal` / all vendors / exclusive to one vendor) and per-partnership pause/resume gives the hiring org full control. Vendors submit candidates on behalf of the partnered org; offers stay internal-only
- **Campus pipelines** — universities issue single-use or cohort invite tokens. Redemption flips a candidate to `kind=student`, links them to the university's pool, and unlocks the AI-driven `/explore` career-path surface
- **Referrals** — any signed-in user mints a "X thought you'd be a fit" share link. Receiving the link reveals the role's full details (the referrer's social signal substitutes for k-anonymity) and creates the application on accept
- **Talent pools** — dynamic segments that auto-populate as candidates match criteria

On `candidate:insert`, the system:
1. **Extracts** a structured profile (skills, experience, education, certifications)
2. **Scores** talent across pillars (technical depth, leadership, communication, domain expertise)
3. **Matches** against every open role, generating fit scores with evidence
4. **Assigns** to matching talent pools
5. **Indexes** for semantic search (so "someone who can lead a distributed team" finds them)

The candidate isn't a resume in a drawer — they're a **living entity** whose signals compound over time.

---

### Act 3: Engage & Attract

The recruiter drafts outreach from the **Compose** interface:

- **Engagement Drafter** agent personalizes messages based on candidate profile + role context
- Multi-channel delivery: email, LinkedIn, SMS
- Response tracking: opened, clicked, replied
- Optimal send timing intelligence
- Silver medalist re-engagement for candidates from prior rounds

Every interaction is tracked. The candidate's engagement history persists across roles.

---

### Act 4: Screen & Assess

Candidates enter the pipeline as **Applications** (created → applied → screening → interview → offer → hired).

**Assessment layer** provides structured evaluation:

| Type | Analyzer | Output |
|------|----------|--------|
| Technical | Subjective (rubric 0-5) | Criterion scores + evidence |
| Aptitude | MCQ (exact match) | Score + breakdown |
| Coding | Code evaluator | Correctness, quality, efficiency |
| Case Study | Rubric-based | Strategic thinking scores |
| Personality | Trait extraction | Behavioral profile |
| Interview Rubric | Transcript analyzer | Per-prompt rating + verbatim evidence quote, scored against rubric from the diarized interview — no candidate submission needed |

Candidates take assessments through the portal — single-question-per-screen, auto-save, timed sessions. On submission, the rules engine auto-evaluates and writes results to the intelligence layer.

**Mock interviews** with AI coaching help candidates prepare — readiness scoring, communication feedback, competency signals. Every interaction makes the candidate better.

---

### Act 5: Interview — The Evidence Room

The interview system is a full **evidence collection pipeline**:

**Before:** `interview.intake` generates a prep plan — evidence targets per competency, suggested questions, candidate-specific talking points.

**During:** The **LiveKit-powered interview room** provides:
- Video grid (candidate + panel)
- Live recording with transcription (agent-dispatched)
- Real-time scorecard capture in the panelist sidebar
- Live assessment delivery during the interview
- Proctoring support
- Data channel sync between participants

**After:** `interview.processEvidence` synthesizes everything:
- Transcript analysis → engagement, depth, communication scores (0-10 each)
- Scorecard alignment → competency-level verdict mapping
- Assessment results → integrated into evaluation
- Red flags and standout moments extracted
- Panel agreement analysis (do interviewers agree?)

The result: a unified `InterviewEvaluation` with evidence chains, not just gut feelings.

---

### Act 6: The Intelligence Synthesis

Every piece of evidence flows into **intelligence tables** — normalized, queryable, compounding:

| Layer | Contains |
|-------|----------|
| **Candidate Intelligence** | Profile summary, talent score (0-100), skill gaps, growth trajectory |
| **Interview Intelligence** | Prep targets, evaluation scores, themes, red flags, standout moments |
| **Assessment Intelligence** | Rubric scores, breakdown, insights, evaluator feedback |
| **Application Intelligence** | Stage progression, decision reasoning, evidence accumulation |
| **Match Intelligence** | Fit score (0-100), criterion alignment with sources, gaps, recommendation |

The **evidence hierarchy** is explicit:
1. Interview alignment (observed behavior)
2. Assessment scores (tested capability)
3. Talent pillars (AI-scored profile)
4. Resume claims (self-reported)

Contradictions between layers are flagged. A candidate who claims "expert Python" but scores 2/5 on a coding assessment gets a gap flag with evidence from both sources.

---

### Act 7: Decide — The Match

The **Role-Candidate Matcher** agent produces the final verdict:

- **Fit Score** (0-100) with evidence chains
- **Criterion Alignment** — per-competency scores with source attribution
- **Signal** — strong / moderate / weak
- **Recommendation** — strong_yes / yes / maybe / no / strong_no
- **Gaps** — what's missing, backed by evidence
- **Summary** — a hiring narrative the recruiter can use in stakeholder conversations

**Decision readiness** is computed automatically — the system signals when enough evidence exists to make a confident hire/pass decision.

---

### Act 8: Close — The Offer

When the decision is made:

- **Offer generation** with compensation snapshot (salary, bonus, equity, benefits)
- **Close risk analysis** — likelihood of acceptance, deal-breakers, timing risks, counter-offer strategy
- **Offer checklist** — completion tracking for onboarding steps
- **Cascade logic** — accepted offer auto-rejects competing applications for the same role
- **Multi-offer compare** — when a candidate holds offers from multiple companies, the portal renders a side-by-side comparison (base, equity, bonus, location, expiry, status) so the candidate can decide with full context

On `offer:resolved`:
- Accepted → candidate status updates to hired, role fulfillment tracked
- Declined → pipeline cascades, candidate returns to pool for future roles

---

### Act 9: The Compounding Loop

This is where Dilliznt diverges from every ATS:

**Nothing is lost.** A candidate who was a "maybe" for Role A is already scored, profiled, and intelligence-enriched when Role B opens. Their interview feedback, assessment scores, and engagement history carry forward.

**The system learns:**
- Talent pools auto-refresh as new candidates match
- Role health analysis tracks pipeline velocity and forecasts time-to-fill
- Recruiter briefings surface daily triage items
- Cross-role learning improves matching accuracy over time

**The candidate grows:**
- Mock interviews and coaching improve readiness
- Portfolio-native profiles (GitHub, papers, certs) replace static resumes
- Skills evolution is tracked, not just captured once

## Part III: The Platform Architecture

### The Four Engines

Dilliznt is built as four interconnected engines, each reinforcing the others:

```
┌─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┐
│                    INTELLIGENCE ENGINE                   │
│         26 specialized AI agents + memory layer          │
├──────────────┬──────────────┬───────────────┬───────────┤
│  SUPPLY      │  DEMAND      │  EVALUATION   │  DECISION │
│  ENGINE      │  ENGINE      │  ENGINE       │  ENGINE   │
│              │              │               │           │
│  Candidates  │  Roles       │  Interviews   │  Matches  │
│  Pools       │  Applications│  Assessments  │  Offers   │
│  Engagement  │  Pipeline    │  Scorecards   │  Triage   │
│  Portal      │  Scheduling  │  Coaching     │  Briefings│
└──────────────┴──────────────┴───────────────┴───────────┘
```

---

### 1. The Intelligence Engine (The Brain)

The core of the platform. Not one general-purpose AI — **26 specialized agents**, each tuned for a single task: resume extraction, talent scoring, matching, interview analysis, offer risk assessment, outreach drafting, and more.

**Agent Families:**

| Family | Agents | Purpose |
|--------|--------|---------|
| **Candidate** | Profile extractor, talent scorer, comparer, interview coach | Understand and develop talent |
| **Role** | Wizard, field enricher, health analyzer, status reporter | Structure and monitor requisitions |
| **Matching** | Role-candidate matcher, pool matcher, pool insight analyzer | Connect supply to demand with evidence |
| **Interview** | Prep planner, transcript analyzer, scorecard analyzer | Extract structured signal from conversations |
| **Assessment** | Evaluator, code evaluator, personality analyzer | Grade and score with rubrics |
| **Offer** | Close analyst, letter generator, letter extractor | Manage the close with risk intelligence |
| **Engagement** | Drafter, briefing generator | Personalize outreach and surface daily priorities |
| **General** | Ask assistant, NL query engine | Ad-hoc questions across the entire talent database |

Every agent produces **structured, validated output** — not free-form text. Scores are numbers. Alignments have evidence chains. Recommendations come with confidence levels.

---

### 2. The Memory Layer (The Nervous System)

The memory layer is what makes Dilliznt compound instead of reset.

**Six intelligence tables** normalize every signal the system collects:

| Layer | What It Captures |
|-------|-----------------|
| **Candidate Intelligence** | Talent score, skill profile, growth trajectory, gaps |
| **Interview Intelligence** | Prep targets, evaluation scores, themes, red flags, standout moments |
| **Assessment Intelligence** | Rubric scores, breakdown by criterion, evaluator insights |
| **Application Intelligence** | Stage progression, decision reasoning, evidence accumulation |
| **Engagement Intelligence** | Response rates, optimal timing, channel effectiveness |
| **Match Intelligence** | Fit score, criterion alignment with source attribution, gaps, recommendation |

**Key property:** Intelligence persists across roles. A candidate interviewed for Role A carries their evaluation into Role B. Assessment scores don't expire. Engagement history informs future outreach tone and timing.

**Evidence hierarchy** ensures the system knows what to trust:

```
Most trusted    ┌──────────────────────────────┐
                │  1. Interview (observed)      │
                │  2. Assessment (tested)       │
                │  3. Talent score (AI-scored)   │
                │  4. Resume (self-reported)     │
Least trusted   └──────────────────────────────┘
```

When sources contradict — "claims expert Python, scored 2/5 on coding assessment" — the system flags the discrepancy with evidence from both sides.

---

### 3. The Automation Layer (The Reflexes)

The platform operates on an **event-driven rules engine**. Every entity change triggers a chain of automated tasks — no manual orchestration required.

**How it works:**
1. A data change occurs (candidate created, interview completed, offer accepted)
2. The event is captured and queued for async processing
3. Rules match the event to the appropriate task chain
4. Tasks execute sequentially — each one feeding the next
5. Results are written to intelligence tables
6. The recruiter sees progress in real time via live streaming updates

**Example chains:**

| Trigger | What Happens Automatically |
|---------|---------------------------|
| New candidate | Parse resume → extract profile → score talent → match against all roles → assign to pools |
| New role | Enrich fields → match against all candidates → identify matching pools |
| Interview completed | Synthesize transcript + scorecards → evaluate competency alignment → update match scores |
| Assessment submitted | Route to correct analyzer → grade → write intelligence → check decision readiness |
| Offer accepted | Update candidate status → reject competing applications → track role fulfillment |

**The recruiter's experience:** they take an action (upload a resume, close an interview), and by the time they navigate to the next page, the system has already profiled, scored, matched, and surfaced what matters. No waiting. No manual steps.

---

### 4. The Search Layer (The Recall)

Three modes of finding talent, each for a different need:

| Mode | How It Works | Best For |
|------|-------------|----------|
| **Browse** | Paginated listing, sorted by recency or score | Daily triage, pipeline review |
| **Keyword search** | Full-text search with stemming and prefix matching across rich fields (resumes, descriptions, notes, transcripts) | Known-name or known-skill lookup |
| **Semantic search** | Natural language queries matched against meaning, not just words | Exploratory sourcing ("someone who can lead a distributed infrastructure team and has scaled systems to 10M+ users") |

**Natural language queries** work across the entire talent database. Recruiters ask questions in plain English and get structured results — no boolean operators, no query syntax, no training.

---

### 5. The Candidate Portal (The Relationship)

Candidates aren't passive entries in a database. They have their own interface:

- **Public opportunities** — anonymous browse before commitment. K-anonymous bucketing means a stranger sees role shape (industry/stage/region/comp band/skills) without learning which company until they sign up
- **Living profiles** that evolve across roles and time. Students and early-career candidates see a kind-aware profile editor (institution, degree, major, GPA, projects, internships, target functions)
- **Assessment portal** — take evaluations with auto-save, progress tracking, and timed sessions
- **Interview prep** — AI coaching with readiness scoring, communication feedback, competency signals
- **Mock interviews** — practice sessions that generate actionable improvement feedback
- **Career path explorer** (`/explore`) — for student/early-career candidates, an AI-driven ranked list of paths with whyItFits, gaps, and time-boxed growth actions. Personalized at render time from the student profile
- **Refer a role** (`/refer`) — issue token-based share links to friends ("X thought you'd be a fit"). Past referrals list with copy-link and revoke
- **Multi-offer compare** — side-by-side decision-room view for candidates juggling offers
- **Feedback visibility** — candidates see where they stand, not just silence

Every portal interaction feeds back into the intelligence layer. A candidate who completes a mock interview and improves their readiness score is a stronger match for the next role — and the system knows it.

---

### 6. The Engagement Layer (The Voice)

Multi-channel, AI-personalized outreach that learns:

- **Drafting** — AI generates personalized messages based on candidate profile + role context + prior interaction history
- **Channels** — email, LinkedIn, SMS
- **Tracking** — opened, clicked, replied
- **Timing** — optimal send windows based on response pattern intelligence
- **Re-engagement** — silver medalist campaigns for prior-round candidates when new matching roles open

### How the Engines Connect

The power of the platform is in the connections, not the individual engines:

```
Supply Engine                    Demand Engine
(candidates, pools)              (roles, applications)
       │                                │
       └──────────┐    ┌────────────────┘
                  ▼    ▼
           Intelligence Engine
          (26 agents + memory)
                  │    │
       ┌──────────┘    └────────────────┐
       ▼                                ▼
Evaluation Engine                Decision Engine
(interviews, assessments)        (matches, offers, triage)
       │                                │
       └──────────┐    ┌────────────────┘
                  ▼    ▼
            Memory Layer
     (compounds across all engines)
```

Every engine feeds the memory layer. The memory layer informs every engine. The result is a system that gets measurably better with every hire — not because of a magic algorithm, but because it never forgets what it learned.
