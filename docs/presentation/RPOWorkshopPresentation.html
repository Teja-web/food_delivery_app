Operating Model · Key Concept

The AI is a sixth actor

"The system doesn't wait for instructions — it reacts to events. When a recruiter uploads a resume, the AI parses, scores, and matches. When a panelist submits a scorecard, the AI synthesizes. Nobody clicks 'run AI.' It just happens."
Operating Model

Handoff points — two parallel tracks

Team Track

  • Recruiter creates role
  • AI generates matches
  • Recruiter reviews & shortlists
  • Recruiter adds to pipeline
  • Recruiter screens
  • Recruiter schedules interview
  • Panelist/HM conducts interview
  • Panelist submits scorecard → AI synthesizes
  • Recruiter assigns assessment → AI evaluates
  • HM makes hire decision → Recruiter builds offer

Candidate Track (Portal)

  • Sees role in Portal when added to pipeline
  • Updates profile
  • Receives interview details
  • Accesses AI prep materials
  • Practices with AI coach
  • Joins interview via Portal/LiveKit
  • Completes assessment in Portal
  • Reviews offer in Portal
  • Accepts/negotiates/declines

The candidate isn't passive — they have their own workspace from the moment they enter the pipeline.

Block 2 · Intake & Setup · 35 min

Intake & Setup

Walk through exactly what happens at the start of a new client engagement — scope, create roles, connect the team.

Intake & Setup

Scope the mandate

Who: Account Lead + Client

Engagement Parameters

  • Number of roles, headcount per role
  • Priority order (which roles to fill first)
  • Target hire dates
  • Compensation ranges and structure
  • Interview process preferences
  • Success criteria (target time-to-hire, fill rate)

Stakeholders & Data Sources

  • Which HM owns which role(s)
  • Who sits on interview panels
  • Escalation contacts on client side
  • Existing ATS? (Greenhouse, Lever, Ashby, Workable)
  • Candidate databases to migrate?
  • Approved sourcing channels

Platform action: Account Lead creates a Department for the client engagement.

Intake & Setup

Create roles

Who: Recruiter (with HM input)

Step 1

Paste the client's JD into Role Wizard

AI extracts: title, criteria with weights, keywords, compensation, requirements. Recruiter reviews and adjusts.

Step 2

Set engagement parameters

Department (links to client), headcount, target hire date, location, employment type, assign Hiring Manager.

Step 3

Configure interview stages

Define stages in order (e.g., Phone Screen → Technical → HM Round). Each stage gets: name, duration, competencies to evaluate.

Step 4

Save the role

AI automatically: enriches missing fields, scores every existing candidate, checks which talent pools align.

Intake & Setup

Role creation — what matters

Don't skip

  • Criteria configuration — drives entire matching and evaluation quality
  • Headcount — don't leave at 1 if multiple openings; affects fill rate tracking
  • Interview stage setup — generates prep materials and structures scorecards

Exercise

  • Create a role using a sample JD
  • Walk through the Role Wizard output together
  • Adjust one criterion weight
  • Configure 3 interview stages
  • Save it — observe AI processing

Source & Match

How the recruiter reviews matches

When: After candidates are loaded (daily during active sourcing)

For each candidate, the recruiter sees:

  • Fit score — overall match quality (0-100)
  • Recommendation — strong yes / yes / maybe / no / strong no
  • Alignment — per-criterion breakdown
  • Signal — single strongest reason for fit
  • Gaps — what's missing or risky
  • Summary — 2-3 sentences ready for HM

Decision framework:

ScoreAction
80-100Add to pipeline immediately
60-79Add, prioritize for screening
40-59Manual review — add if pipeline thin
<40Dismiss (won't resurface for this role)

"Add to Pipeline" = one click → Application at Sourced stage → Candidate Portal activates

Source & Match · The Shift

What the recruiter is not doing

  • Not reading every resume from scratch
  • Not manually cross-referencing JDs against profiles
  • Not writing up screening notes from memory
  • Not guessing which candidates to prioritize

Their time goes to judgment calls, not mechanical work.

Source & Match

Talent pools — the long-term asset

Pools are persistent candidate segments that outlive individual roles.

Example

Senior Java Engineers — Hyderabad

Reusable pipeline across multiple client roles

Example

Product Managers — Remote

Ready bench for future mandates

Example

Alumni — Previously Placed

Referral network, repeat placements

How they work:

  • Recruiter defines pool criteria (skills, locations, experience)
  • System auto-populates with matching candidates
  • New roles matching pool criteria → candidates surface immediately
  • New candidates matching pool criteria → added automatically
"A candidate dismissed for one role is still in the pool. Pre-qualified for the next similar role. Nothing is wasted."
Source & Match

The Sourced stage: screening

Who: Recruiter  ·  When: Immediately after adding to pipeline

Review

Full profile, match context, and evidence

Everything the AI gathered — structured and ready to read.

Screen

Conduct phone screen or initial assessment

Document notes in the platform (entity notes).

Decide

Advance, Reject, or Hold

Advance → move to Interview (triggers match re-evaluation, assessment auto-assignment)
Reject → captures reason (feeds source effectiveness metrics)
Hold → leave in Sourced, revisit later

Exercise: Walk through Matches tab. Review 3-4 records. Add one to pipeline. Dismiss one. Discuss what information drove the decision.

Block 4 · Execute · 40 min

Execute

The daily operational rhythm for each persona — interview, assess, and close.

Execute · Recruiter

A day in the life: Morning

Open Today dashboard (30 min)

  • Open roles with pipeline stage counts
  • Today's scheduled interviews
  • Triage items — AI-generated action items
  • Daily briefing — AI summary of what needs attention

Review overnight activity

  • New candidates loaded? Check match scores
  • Assessments completed? Review AI evaluations
  • Interview intelligence ready? Review transcript analysis and scorecard synthesis

Triage examples: "Candidate X in Sourced for 7 days", "Interview with Y needs scheduling", "Assessment for Z is overdue"

Execute · Recruiter

A day in the life: Midday & EOD

Schedule & process interviews

  • Select candidate, role, stage → propose time slots
  • System checks panelist calendar availability
  • Confirm and send invitations
  • Interview Prep auto-generates for panelist
  • Candidate receives prep materials + AI coach access in Portal
  • Review AI-generated evaluation (competency scores from transcript)
  • Review panelist scorecards
  • Compare AI analysis with human judgment
  • Make advancement decision

End of day: Pipeline hygiene

  • Move candidates through stages based on day's decisions
  • Follow up on pending items from triage
  • Check stalled candidates (days in stage)
Key insight

Where AI and human agree = high confidence.
Where they disagree = deeper review needed.

Execute · Hiring Manager

A day in the life: Hiring Manager

Before

Receives AI-generated interview prep

Who the candidate is, what to evaluate, specific questions to ask, scoring guidance (what 1, 3, and 5 looks like)

During

Conducts interview via LiveKit video room

Recording and transcript captured automatically. Can take notes during interview.

After

Submits scorecard

Rate each competency with evidence notes. Overall rating + verdict. AI processes immediately upon submit.

Decide

Makes the hire decision with full picture

Match score (with interview + assessment evidence), panel consensus, assessment results, AI recommendation.

Execute

The offer flow

Step 1-3

Initiate

HM approves hire → Recruiter moves to Offer stage → AI generates close intelligence (acceptance likelihood, risk factors, candidate motivators, negotiation strategy)

Step 4-7

Build

Recruiter reviews intelligence → Sets comp terms (base, equity, bonus, start date) → AI generates offer letter from org identity + role + candidate → Optional: extract candidate's current comp for negotiation context

Step 8-9

Extend

Candidate sees offer in portal → Accept, negotiate, or decline

Outcomes

Track everything

Accepted: Mark hired, update fill count  |  Negotiation: Adjust terms, new version with change tracking  |  Declined: Capture reason, feeds analytics

System tracks: every offer version, trigger type, risk flags (parity, budget, band compliance), full audit trail.

Reference

Objection handling

ObjectionResponse
"AI can't replace recruiter judgment""It gives recruiters better candidates faster. You spend time on judgment calls — not on the 4 hours of screening to find 3 people worth talking to."
"Our clients won't trust AI scores""Clients see the same evidence the recruiter sees — criterion-by-criterion alignment with source attribution. It's more transparent than any recruiter summary."
"We already have an ATS""Dilliznt can integrate with the existing ATS if provided access. It is however designed to act independently as an execution engine, not a tracking system."
"Is candidate data shared?""Yes — deliberately. Learnings from one engagement translate to meaningful signals across all. A candidate evaluated for Client A is pre-qualified intelligence for Client B. That's the compounding advantage."
"What about our client data?""Roles, pipelines, offers, client details, interview content — all org-private. No cross-client visibility. Only candidate intelligence compounds across engagements."
"What's the ramp-up cost?""Minimal disruption. Roles live in Week 1, AI-matched pipeline in Week 2, first hire by Week 5. Recruiters learn by doing — the system guides the workflow."
"What if the AI is wrong?""Recruiter and HM always make the final call. And when they override, that's data too — the system learns from disagreement and improves."
"Adoption resistance""The system removes drudgery, not autonomy. Recruiters who use it close faster and handle more roles — it's a leverage tool, not a replacement."
